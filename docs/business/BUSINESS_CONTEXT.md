## 4. Problem and answer

**Problem.** Agents pick models blind. They use whatever they were configured with, even though the right choice depends on the task, available hardware and providers, cost, and policy (licence, model origin, data residency), all of which change weekly. Businesses overspend on inference, run models they aren't allowed to use, and miss better options.

**Answer.** An environment-aware, unbiased model decision. The agent (or its harness) sends hardware, available providers, use case and policy, and gets back ranked models with scores, cost and reasons, plus per-subtask routing advice and alerts when something better ships.

**One-sentence test:** *Environment, use case and policy in; ranked models with reasons out.*

---

## 5. Positioning: "unbiased"

- Routers earn money on the inference they route, so they have an incentive to steer traffic. ModelSpec earns only on the decision.
- Agents can't judge "unbiased" from marketing, so it must be **machine-checkable**:
  - No provider referral fees or paid placement, stated in listings and docs.
  - Every response carries evidence: sources, dates, method version.
  - Signed responses, so results can be audited later.
  - Published ranking method, even if live data is paid.
- Competitors and adjacent players (as of the conversation): Artificial Analysis, OpenRouter (including auto-routing), LMArena, models.dev (open source, used inside coding agents), Epoch AI, Hugging Face leaderboards, auto model modes in harnesses.
- Why frontier labs won't build this: a lab won't ship a neutral "use a competitor" recommender. The real threat is routers and harnesses shipping "good enough" picking.
- **Implication of cancelling latency:** ModelSpec now aggregates mostly public evidence, like several competitors. Differentiation must come from constraint-aware decisions (hardware, policy, origin, licence), evidence quality, neutrality, and data generated by use (section 9).

## 6. Product: planned endpoints

Split by call frequency so revenue doesn't depend on one rarely called endpoint.

| Endpoint | Frequency | Purpose | Draft price (estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `recommend` | Rare, high value (setup, new model releases) | Ranked models + reasons for an environment + use case + policy | $0.05–0.25 per successful result |
| `route` | Per subtask, frequent | Cheapest model adequate for this step, based on public evidence, cost and constraints | ~$0.001 per call |
| `policy-check` | Every deploy or model change | Compliance gate: licence, origin, residency | Included in Team/Enterprise |
| `watch` | Subscription | Alerts when a better or cheaper option appears | Included in Team |

Note: `route` has lost its planned speed advantage (latency data). Validate demand before investing heavily in it; `recommend`, `policy-check` and `watch` are now the core.

**Environment detection (CLI side).** GPU/VRAM/RAM; installed runtimes (Ollama, vLLM, llama.cpp); which provider API keys are **present** (never read or send values); an optional policy file. Output is ranked JSON with reasons an agent can act on.

**Premium idea.** Run a quick eval on a customer's own sample tasks to confirm a pick, because public benchmarks predict poorly for specific tasks. Only offer this if it can be run reliably; the latency cancellation shows testing quality must come first.

**Benchgraph's role.** The evidence layer and credibility/SEO engine behind ModelSpec, not a separately sold product. Avoid paid "verified benchmark" listings (conflict of interest). Its coverage quality is now more important, since public evidence is the main input.

## 7. Agent-to-agent commerce (founder's preferred channel)

**Facts gathered 2026-09-16 (verify, fast-moving):**
- x402 (HTTP 402 stablecoin micropayments) was handed to a Linux Foundation–governed x402 Foundation in April 2026; Visa and Stripe have integrated with it. Google's AP2 handles proving a human authorized spending; x402 moves the money.
- Adoption: ~165–167M x402 transactions by Q1/April 2026, but one tracker estimated real commerce at only ~$28K/day, with ~50% of activity gamified/testing.
- Sources: presenc.ai/research/x402-protocol-adoption-tracker-2026, rzlt.io/blog/agentic-payments-2026-x402-explainer, orbilontech.com/ai-agent-payments-ap2-x402, chainalysis.com/blog/x402-agentic-payments-adoption

**Implication.** The rails are real; autonomous agent spending is still small. $10K MRR from agent micropayments alone means ~40K paid `recommend` results/month at $0.25, or ~200K at $0.05. Build for it; don't depend on it for year one.

**Chosen approach: one system, both channels.**
1. **x402 from day one**, so wallet-holding agents can discover, pay and use it with no human.
2. **Card-funded API keys with daily caps**, because humans funding agents are where most year-one money comes from.
3. **Charge per successful result**, never for errors.
4. **Free sandbox** (e.g. keys starting `test_` return realistic fake results).
5. **Receipts per call:** time, input in plain words, result, cost, and **estimated savings** (e.g. "recommended X over Y, ~$340/month less at your volume").

## 8. Pricing and revenue model

**Structure**
- Free: open-source CLI, delayed data snapshot, sandbox.
- Usage: per successful result via card keys or x402.
- Team: ~$49–99/month (policy checks, drift alerts, saved org policies, higher caps).
- Enterprise governance: ~$1–5K/month (approved-model registry, audit trails, licence/origin/residency checks). Highest willingness to pay but 3–6 month sales cycles. **Now the strongest revenue lever.**
- Data licensing: a live, verified, normalized model-and-policy dataset (licence, origin, residency, hardware fit) to gateways, observability tools and harnesses. Harder to sell than the latency feed would have been, because free sources like models.dev exist; must win on verification, policy fields and freshness.
- Later: per-adopted-recommendation or savings-linked pricing, once receipts prove savings.

**Do not:** charge per local CLI call (unmeterable); sell ranking placement; take undisclosed referral fees.

**Paths to $10K MRR (assumptions)**
- Self-serve only: ~130 Team customers at $79. Hard; dev tools convert free users at low single-digit rates.
- **Mixed (most realistic):** 3 enterprise at ~$2K + ~50 Team.
- One data-licensing deal: less likely now without proprietary measurement data.
- Usage-based API revenue alone probably won't carry year one.

## 9. Moats: be first at things that compound

1. **Outcome data (now the primary data moat).** With permission, log which recommendations were adopted and whether tasks succeeded. Over time this shows what works in practice, not just what scores well, and no aggregator has it. Design consent and privacy in from the start.
2. **Harness defaults.** Being the built-in model picker in one popular harness beats many registry listings. Integrations are sticky.
3. **Trust record.** Signed, timestamped recommendations create a public history of being right.
4. **Verified, policy-rich graph.** Human-reviewed evidence plus normalized licence, origin, residency and hardware-fit fields that competitors don't maintain. Only defensible if kept accurate and current.
5. **Search/name ownership.** Task-worded pages ("which model should my agent use").

**Licensing (proposed, not final)**
- CLI: stays open source (distribution).
- Graph data: free but delayed (e.g. monthly snapshot); live verified feed paid.
- Outcome data: proprietary, aggregated and anonymized.
- Keep contributor agreements and document data-source terms (check terms of any scraped benchmark sites) so IP is transferable.

## 10. Exit strategy

**Target: ~$500K sale in 12–24 months (+2–4 months to close).**
- **Revenue-based sale (now the main path):** ~$100–170K ARR at ~3–5x for small profitable software (rough range).
- Acqui-hire plus assets: faster, usually smaller.
- Data/asset sale: less likely without a proprietary measurement dataset; becomes viable only if outcome data or harness usage is substantial.
- Channels: direct to integration partners (usually better price), or marketplaces/brokers such as Acquire.com.
- Rough net after taxes and fees: ~$350–425K (not financial advice; depends on structure and jurisdiction).
- Negotiate handover up front; 3–6 months is normal at this size.

**"Few million" upside** requires a strategic buyer: distribution (harness integrations, real agent usage), proprietary outcome data, and a demonstrated team. Likely buyers: AI gateways, routers, observability, agent infrastructure. Selling to a router ends neutrality. Larger deals often include 1–3 year retention and earnouts.

**Engineering implications of "sellable":**
- Low running costs, high margins.
- **Runs without the founder:** automated data refresh, documented ops, no single-person steps.
- Clean, documented data pipeline and IP ownership.
- Recurring revenue (subscriptions, licences) over one-off spikes.

## 13. Reference: agent-callable product playbook (external guide)

A guide the founder shared (from X) recommends: build a single-job API endpoint; validate by asking "does it make the business money?" and "is it too narrow for a lab to build?"; research demand in skill registries (skills.sh, agentskills.io, ClawHub) and niche communities; agent-first docs with a "use when" sentence; per-business API key, daily spend cap and plain-language receipt; sandbox mode; registry listings; getting into harnesses as a default; one search-optimized page per endpoint; start per call, move to per result or monthly.

**Assessment against ModelSpec:** passes the money test (measurable savings) and the lab test (labs won't recommend competitors); passes the one-sentence test; **weak on call frequency** (model choice is occasional), hence the endpoint split in section 6. The guide likely overstates autonomous registry discovery and agents holding their own cards today; its advice about harness defaults is the most valuable part.

**Draft registry description (<60 words):**
> Ranks AI models you can actually run, given your hardware, providers, use case and policy rules. Use when choosing, switching, or checking a model before a task or deploy. Returns ranked models with scores, cost, and reasons; on failure, returns which constraint eliminated every option.
